# Decision cases from additional transcript review

These are generalized teaching situations, not quotations or independently verified causal findings. Keep actual source records private. A call can contain both excellent discovery and a missed opportunity.

## Buyer corrects the diagnosis
A buyer says reports already arrive, after the rep repeatedly asks about missing reports. Update the diagnosis immediately. Do not continue a pain-question sequence on a premise the buyer rejected.
Suggested response: “Thanks for correcting me. The reports exist; the issue is keeping the locations running consistently without you. Can you walk me through the last time you stepped away and what broke down?”
Evaluate whether the next question uses the correction. If connection problems or a driving buyer are mentioned, offer a better time rather than attributing the ending solely to frustration.

## Buyer wants implementation, not more knowledge
Distinguish a plan, execution support, and done-for-you delivery. If the buyer says earlier information contradicts today's pitch, repair the discrepancy before another feature explanation.
Suggested response: “You heard something different earlier. Let me verify exactly what is included and what would be separate. What would someone need to do alongside your team for this to meet your need?”
Do not promise unverified support. Escalate a factual question to the appropriate owner; do not invent an answer or guarantee a solved constraint.

## Prior bad experience and upsell concern
A buyer describes paying for industry expertise that did not materialize, or attending an event dominated by upsells. Generic claims of expertise do not address this. Ask which evidence and delivery boundaries would matter; use only approved relevant evidence. Acknowledge a clear refusal and end respectfully.
Suggested response: “What you want to avoid is paying for one outcome and discovering you need another purchase. Here is what this purchase includes [verified scope], and what it does not. Would reviewing that help, or is this simply not a fit?”

## Cash and capacity are real constraints
When the buyer describes break-even operations, equipment needs, and limited capacity, do not turn every constraint into a mindset objection. Explore fit without pushing financing or projecting hypothetical ROI. A helpful resource and permission-based follow-up may be the appropriate result.

## Strong discovery still needs accurate offer facts
A useful positive pattern is asking about the bottleneck, understanding why it persists, summarizing it in the buyer's words, getting explicit correction/confirmation, and connecting the offer to that diagnosis. Keep the uncertainty the buyer expressed; do not imply a general workshop can guarantee resolution of regulatory or industry-specific problems. Verify dates and policy questions instead of guessing.

## Outcome ladder
- Buyer intends to pay later: commitment; payment unverified.
- Buyer says payment submitted: buyer-reported submission.
- Rep explicitly confirms receipt: payment reported received on call; ledger unverified.
- Rep confirms first installment: partial payment reported; remaining balance pending.
- Authoritative transaction/deal record matched and dated: independently verified outcome, with amount/status as actually recorded.
Never collapse these categories. A deposit is not full payment; a booked next step is not a loss. Do not copy payment credentials into coaching reports.

## Practice and reassessment
Choose one skill, not an entire rewritten script. Run a three-turn role-play: buyer concern, rep response, buyer correction. Assess whether the rep acknowledges the correction, asks a relevant question, uses truthful offer facts, and proposes an appropriate next step. Repeat with a harder variant. On the next real call look for the same observable behavior; do not promise a conversion increase.
