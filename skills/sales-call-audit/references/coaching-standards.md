# Coaching standards

These are an operational adaptation of training, not a predictive scoring model. Source IDs H1, V1, V2, and T1 are resolved in the accompanying brand source profile. The owner can replace that profile without changing this reusable skill.

## Frame the conversation (H1: Introduction)

Check clear identity, company, reason for contact, applicable recording disclosure, and an agreed agenda. Separate the organization's stated practice from universal legal requirements. Establish available time early. A rushed or uninvited pitch can undermine the rest of the call; a short opener should still orient the buyer.

## Diagnose before prescribing (H1: Discovery; V1: 3507–3812 seconds)

Establish current situation and desired result. Explore the obstacle, why prior attempts have not worked, and specific examples when answers are vague. Recap using the buyer's language, map that problem to a solution category the offer actually covers, then confirm understanding. Repeat only as needed. End discovery with a buyer-confirmed recap rather than assuming agreement from silence.

Look for leading questions that substitute the rep's diagnosis for the buyer's answer. If the buyer corrects the rep, carry that correction through the rest of the audit. Do not mechanically force more pain questions when the buyer has already identified a clear, relevant problem.

## Make the offer relevant (H1: Offer)

After the recap, transition with permission. Map each material buyer problem to a real feature/solution, a truthful reason to trust it, and a relevant benefit. Confirm whether that connection helps. Avoid a generic tour of features or unsupported promises. Ask clearly for the appropriate commitment and give the buyer room to respond. The training's compact offer structure is a coaching aid, not a universal two-minute limit for all sales motions.

## Understand the objection (H1: Objections; V2: 973–1083 and 6235–6296 seconds)

Classify the buyer's stated concern and preserve their actual wording:

| Family | Diagnose | Useful next move |
|---|---|---|
| Time/timing | Implementation capacity, calendar logistics, or priority? | Clarify which constraint applies and what would need to change. |
| Money | Affordability, cash timing, value, or perceived risk? | Isolate the concern; connect only substantiated benefits to the actual cost. |
| Decision-maker | Approval, shared decision, or an implementer who needs to attend? | Establish who needs involvement and arrange the right conversation. |
| Preference/fit | Specific feature mismatch, uncertainty about method, or genuine disqualification? | Explain the relevant capability honestly and confirm fit. |
| Stall | Unspecified uncertainty or a concrete information gap? | Ask what remains unresolved and what decision criteria matter. |

Multiple categories may apply. Track trust/proof requests separately even if mapped to preference/value. Do not treat every logistical objection as a fake excuse. Before the offer, address known obstacles during discovery rather than saving them for a surprise at the close.

## Loop without arguing (H1: Looping; V1: 4564–4789, 5297–5398 seconds)

First acknowledge the concern and verify whether the buyer sees value; triage the main unresolved issue. Then acknowledge, address the actual issue, and invite the appropriate next step. Record the buyer's reply before judging whether the objection was resolved. A polite acknowledgement is not buying intent.

The video describes acknowledge–associate–ask; the handbook describes acknowledge–address–ask after value confirmation. Keep those variants distinct. Association can normalize a reasonable concern, but do not invent another customer's results or an authority conversation. A new ask must remain responsive to the buyer and the offer; endless repetition is not evidence of skilled coaching. Recognize a clear no, lack of authority, or lack of fit.

## Finish with a concrete next step (H1: BAMFAM)

When a decision depends on time, another participant, or unresolved information, aim to agree a follow-up while still speaking. Specify who attends, what must happen beforehand, when to reconnect, and what decision the next call serves. Check actual agreement and receipt where observable. An offer to send a resource is not an appointment; an appointment is not a purchase.

## Delivery and practice (H1: Tone; T1; V2: 6176–6218 seconds)

Separate wording from delivery. Review audibility, clarity, pace, pitch, and pauses only with usable audio or reliable measured evidence. A timestamped transcript may support speaking-time estimates, not conclusions about vocal confidence. The source's 150–170 WPM range is a training reference, not a validated universal pass/fail cutoff. Do not repeat the source's claimed conversion lifts as established evidence without verification.

Coach one behavior at a time. Pick a short real moment, role-play the buyer's concern, have the rep try a more relevant question or response, and repeat until they can execute without losing the substance. Measure that behavior in subsequent calls rather than promising a close-rate increase.
