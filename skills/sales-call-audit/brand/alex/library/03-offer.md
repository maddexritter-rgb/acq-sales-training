# Offer, evidence, and fit

Original coaching adaptations. Suggested dialogue is newly written, not a source quotation. Source ranges identify the reviewed teaching sections supporting this module. Each card has a distinct trigger; apply only when the call evidence fits.



## OFF01 — Build the offer from the problem

**Notice:** The pitch could be delivered unchanged to any buyer.

**Change:** Connect the confirmed problem to an actual capability and the resulting benefit.

**Suggested wording:** “The issue is inconsistent onboarding. [Verified capability] addresses that by [specific mechanism]. Is that the part that matters most?”

**Next branch:** If relevant, explain the next component; if not, revisit the diagnosis.

**Practice check:** Every offered feature has a buyer-specific reason.

**Boundary:** Do not invent a capability to make the mapping work.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 922–976; V2 lines 5229–5457 (10101.1–10524.8s); V2 lines 6001–6500 (11574.7–12574.8s)



## OFF02 — Explain the result before the mechanics

**Notice:** The rep lists modules and jargon without explaining their purpose.

**Change:** Describe what the buyer can do differently, then disclose the necessary work.

**Suggested wording:** “The point is a repeatable handoff. The sessions are how the team builds and tests it.”

**Next branch:** If the buyer asks how, show the mechanism; if they ask effort, answer directly.

**Practice check:** Translate one feature into a concrete change without promising an unverified result.

**Boundary:** Outcome language must not conceal delivery limits.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 922–976; V2 lines 5229–5457 (10101.1–10524.8s); V2 lines 6001–6500 (11574.7–12574.8s)



## OFF03 — Use an analogy to clarify

**Notice:** A complex mechanism is difficult to understand.

**Change:** Use a short original comparison, then return to the actual service.

**Suggested wording:** “Think of it as a checklist at the handoff: it makes the missing step visible. The actual deliverable is [verified item].”

**Next branch:** If the analogy helps, move on; if confusing, explain plainly.

**Practice check:** The buyer can state the real mechanism after the analogy.

**Boundary:** An analogy is not evidence of effectiveness.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 922–976; V2 lines 5229–5457 (10101.1–10524.8s); V2 lines 6001–6500 (11574.7–12574.8s)



## OFF04 — Match proof to the concern

**Notice:** An unrelated testimonial or company statistic substitutes for answering the concern.

**Change:** Choose verified evidence involving the relevant problem, context, and limits.

**Suggested wording:** “The relevant example is [approved evidence]. Its similarity is [fact], and its limitation is [difference].”

**Next branch:** If relevant evidence is absent, say so and explain what can be verified.

**Practice check:** Explain why one proof point is relevant instead of stacking status claims.

**Boundary:** Never fabricate a customer story or imply typical results from one case.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 922–976; V2 lines 5229–5457 (10101.1–10524.8s); V2 lines 6001–6500 (11574.7–12574.8s)



## OFF05 — Keep the explanation short enough to discuss

**Notice:** The rep gives a long uninterrupted feature tour.

**Change:** Explain one relevant component and check the buyer's response before continuing.

**Suggested wording:** “That covers the hiring part. Does that address the issue described, or is the training gap more important?”

**Next branch:** If understood, proceed; if not, clarify that part.

**Practice check:** Pause for a substantive buyer response between major components.

**Boundary:** The source's three-minute pitch is a guide, not a universal cutoff.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 922–976; V2 lines 5229–5457 (10101.1–10524.8s); V2 lines 6001–6500 (11574.7–12574.8s)



## OFF06 — Give known factual answers

**Notice:** The buyer asks a concrete question about terms or delivery.

**Change:** Answer the known fact first, then ask why it matters if useful.

**Suggested wording:** “The confirmed support period is [approved term]. What needs to happen during that period for this to fit?”

**Next branch:** If unknown, verify; if the requirement exceeds scope, identify the mismatch.

**Practice check:** Answer the question before returning to discovery.

**Boundary:** Do not use questions to evade refund, price, or scope information.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 922–976; V2 lines 5229–5457 (10101.1–10524.8s); V2 lines 6001–6500 (11574.7–12574.8s)



## OFF07 — Check implementation feasibility

**Notice:** The offer sounds attractive but requires time or resources the buyer lacks.

**Change:** State the actual work required and ask how it would get done.

**Suggested wording:** “This requires [verified effort]. Who would own that work, and is the capacity available?”

**Next branch:** If capacity exists, plan ownership; if not, consider a different option or timing.

**Practice check:** Name an implementation owner and realistic capacity before claiming fit.

**Boundary:** Do not promise effortless implementation.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 922–976; V2 lines 5229–5457 (10101.1–10524.8s); V2 lines 6001–6500 (11574.7–12574.8s)



## OFF08 — Compare alternatives on buyer criteria

**Notice:** The buyer is considering another provider or doing it internally.

**Change:** Ask which criteria matter and compare verified facts against them.

**Suggested wording:** “What must the chosen option do that the current approach cannot?”

**Next branch:** If the alternative fits better, acknowledge it; if uncertain, identify the information needed.

**Practice check:** Use the same criteria for all options, including doing nothing.

**Boundary:** Do not invent competitor weaknesses or claim an inevitable result.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 922–976; V2 lines 5229–5457 (10101.1–10524.8s); V2 lines 6001–6500 (11574.7–12574.8s)



## OFF09 — Resolve contradictory service information

**Notice:** The buyer says a prior rep described a different offer.

**Change:** Acknowledge the inconsistency and verify the prior statement and current scope before persuading.

**Suggested wording:** “Those descriptions conflict. The team should reconcile them before asking for a decision.”

**Next branch:** If current scope is verified, explain it clearly; if not, schedule only a factual clarification if wanted.

**Practice check:** Produce one consistent written scope before another buying request.

**Boundary:** A confident longer explanation does not resolve contradictory evidence.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 922–976; V2 lines 5229–5457 (10101.1–10524.8s); V2 lines 6001–6500 (11574.7–12574.8s)



## OFF10 — Tie price to agreed scope

**Notice:** The rep presents a number without connecting it to the actual purchase.

**Change:** Summarize deliverables, effort, duration, and total terms using approved facts.

**Suggested wording:** “For [confirmed scope], the total is [approved amount], with [terms]. Would it help to compare the available options?”

**Next branch:** If scope is wrong, correct it first; if price is the concern, diagnose it.

**Practice check:** The buyer can identify what the price covers and excludes.

**Boundary:** Check arithmetic; do not reproduce erroneous source calculations or guaranteed outcome timelines.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 922–976; V2 lines 5229–5457 (10101.1–10524.8s); V2 lines 6001–6500 (11574.7–12574.8s)
