# Diagnose the concern before responding

Original coaching adaptations. Suggested dialogue is newly written, not a source quotation. Source ranges identify the reviewed teaching sections supporting this module. Each card has a distinct trigger; apply only when the call evidence fits.



## OBJ01 — A reaction is not yet an objection

**Notice:** The buyer says that is expensive or pauses without rejecting the offer.

**Change:** Allow the buyer to finish and ask what the reaction means.

**Suggested wording:** “What is the main concern with that price?”

**Next branch:** If value, explore expected benefit; if affordability, discuss fit and approved options.

**Practice check:** Obtain the concern before giving a rebuttal.

**Boundary:** Do not treat an absent response on a bad line as resistance.

## OBJ02 — Confirm the concern accurately

**Notice:** The rep answers a different objection from the one stated.

**Change:** Briefly restate the concern before offering information.

**Suggested wording:** “The concern is paying again without implementation support. Is that right?”

**Next branch:** If confirmed, address that risk; if corrected, change the response.

**Practice check:** One accurate restatement precedes the explanation.

**Boundary:** Do not relabel a clear requirement as fear or lack of belief.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 977–1207; V1 lines 2226–2337 (4564.0–4788.8s); V1 lines 2716–2742 (5487.1–5547.8s); V2 lines 5501–5582 (10605.2–10753.4s)



## OBJ03 — Find the unresolved decision

**Notice:** The buyer says they need to think without identifying a topic.

**Change:** Ask what needs to become clear for a decision.

**Suggested wording:** “What part needs more thought: the fit, the commitment, or something else?”

**Next branch:** If specific, address it; if they want private time, respect that and offer an optional follow-up.

**Practice check:** Name the unresolved question or accept the request for time.

**Boundary:** The buyer does not owe a hidden objection.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 977–1207; V1 lines 2226–2337 (4564.0–4788.8s); V1 lines 2716–2742 (5487.1–5547.8s); V2 lines 5501–5582 (10605.2–10753.4s)



## OBJ04 — Check whether the offer is wanted

**Notice:** The rep negotiates payment while the buyer does not see a useful solution.

**Change:** Separate desire for the solution from its terms.

**Suggested wording:** “Leaving the payment structure aside for a moment, does the actual service solve the right problem?”

**Next branch:** If no, revisit fit; if yes, diagnose terms.

**Practice check:** Avoid payment negotiation before basic fit is established.

**Boundary:** A hypothetical yes is not permission to charge.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 977–1207; V1 lines 2226–2337 (4564.0–4788.8s); V1 lines 2716–2742 (5487.1–5547.8s); V2 lines 5501–5582 (10605.2–10753.4s)



## OBJ05 — Separate multiple concerns

**Notice:** Several concerns emerge and the rep answers whichever is easiest.

**Change:** List them briefly and ask which needs resolving first.

**Suggested wording:** “There are two issues: attendance and ongoing support. Which should be settled first?”

**Next branch:** If one blocks everything, handle it first; if either is a fit failure, do not stack closes.

**Practice check:** Keep a visible list of unresolved issues and mark only buyer-confirmed resolutions.

**Boundary:** Do not use isolation to demand a premature buying promise.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 977–1207; V1 lines 2226–2337 (4564.0–4788.8s); V1 lines 2716–2742 (5487.1–5547.8s); V2 lines 5501–5582 (10605.2–10753.4s)



## OBJ06 — Acknowledge without invented association

**Notice:** The rep tries to build agreement by claiming other clients felt the same.

**Change:** Acknowledge the concern directly; use a real approved example only if available.

**Suggested wording:** “It makes sense to check who will do the work after the meeting. Here is the confirmed scope.”

**Next branch:** If the buyer accepts the answer, ask the appropriate next question; otherwise clarify.

**Practice check:** No invented customer or personal experience appears in the response.

**Boundary:** The video's association step is not permission to fabricate social proof.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 977–1207; V1 lines 2226–2337 (4564.0–4788.8s); V1 lines 2716–2742 (5487.1–5547.8s); V2 lines 5501–5582 (10605.2–10753.4s)



## OBJ07 — Ask permission before challenging a frame

**Notice:** The buyer's assumption may be incomplete but the rep becomes argumentative.

**Change:** Ask permission to offer a factual alternative, then check it.

**Suggested wording:** “Would it help to look at one other way of comparing the options?”

**Next branch:** If yes, show the relevant fact; if no, stop pushing the reframe.

**Practice check:** One offered perspective followed by a buyer response.

**Boundary:** Do not shame spending choices or attack identity.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 977–1207; V1 lines 2226–2337 (4564.0–4788.8s); V1 lines 2716–2742 (5487.1–5547.8s); V2 lines 5501–5582 (10605.2–10753.4s)



## OBJ08 — Check whether the answer resolved anything

**Notice:** The rep delivers a rebuttal and immediately starts another.

**Change:** Ask whether the information addresses the specific concern.

**Suggested wording:** “Does that answer the support question, or is something still missing?”

**Next branch:** If resolved, invite the next step; if not, investigate what remains.

**Practice check:** The buyer, not the rep, confirms resolution.

**Boundary:** Do not assume silence or politeness means agreement.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 977–1207; V1 lines 2226–2337 (4564.0–4788.8s); V1 lines 2716–2742 (5487.1–5547.8s); V2 lines 5501–5582 (10605.2–10753.4s)



## OBJ09 — Re-ask only after progress

**Notice:** The rep repeats the buying request without adding useful information.

**Change:** Make another stage-appropriate invitation only after a concern is addressed and the buyer remains engaged.

**Suggested wording:** “With that clarified, would the next meeting be useful?”

**Next branch:** If ready, act; if not, identify the remaining issue or respect the decline.

**Practice check:** Each renewed ask follows a specific resolved concern.

**Boundary:** Repeated asks alone are not a coaching technique.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 977–1207; V1 lines 2226–2337 (4564.0–4788.8s); V1 lines 2716–2742 (5487.1–5547.8s); V2 lines 5501–5582 (10605.2–10753.4s)



## OBJ10 — Recognize a useful no

**Notice:** The buyer clearly declines, opts out, or has an unmet must-have.

**Change:** Confirm the mismatch or decision and close respectfully.

**Suggested wording:** “It sounds like this does not fit the support needed. Thanks for clarifying that.”

**Next branch:** If the buyer requests an alternative, discuss verified options; if not, end.

**Practice check:** Do not add a new sales loop after a clear final refusal.

**Boundary:** Not every lost sale is a rep failure.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 977–1207; V1 lines 2226–2337 (4564.0–4788.8s); V1 lines 2716–2742 (5487.1–5547.8s); V2 lines 5501–5582 (10605.2–10753.4s)
