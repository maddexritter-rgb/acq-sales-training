# Opportunity flow and measurement

Original coaching adaptations. Suggested dialogue is newly written, not a source quotation. Source ranges identify the reviewed teaching sections supporting this module. Each card has a distinct trigger; apply only when the call evidence fits.



## OPS01 — Diagnose the whole funnel

**Notice:** A lower close percentage is treated as proof the team deteriorated.

**Change:** Track booked, showed, qualified, offered, purchased, and collected with explicit denominators.

**Suggested wording:** “The team should compare total qualified sales and cash alongside close percentage.”

**Next branch:** If volume or mix changed, analyze that change; if stable, investigate conversion behavior.

**Practice check:** Report counts and denominators for the same cohort.

**Boundary:** Do not add percentage improvements that should compound multiplicatively.

## OPS02 — Compare comparable calls

**Notice:** A leaderboard mixes warm existing customers with cold prospects.

**Change:** Separate stage, source, offer, buyer readiness, and rep before drawing a pattern.

**Suggested wording:** “Compare these calls with others serving the same purpose and similar lead context.”

**Next branch:** If comparable samples are small, state that; if sufficient, report observed patterns.

**Practice check:** Use n of N applicable calls and retain exceptions.

**Boundary:** No employee ranking from a single call.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 421–550; V1 lines 1–622 (0.0–1298.3s); V2 lines 3967–4725 (7800.6–9138.6s); V2 lines 7001–7766 (13624.4–15188.8s)



## OPS03 — Reduce unnecessary scheduling delay

**Notice:** An interested buyer is booked far out despite nearer availability.

**Change:** Offer actual earlier openings without canceling the original slot until agreed.

**Suggested wording:** “An earlier opening is available. Would today or the existing time work better?”

**Next branch:** If earlier works, reconfirm details; if not, keep the agreed appointment.

**Practice check:** The buyer chooses the new time and understands the change.

**Boundary:** Fifteen-minute start increments do not imply fifteen-minute sales calls.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 421–550; V1 lines 1–622 (0.0–1298.3s); V2 lines 3967–4725 (7800.6–9138.6s); V2 lines 7001–7766 (13624.4–15188.8s)



## OPS04 — Make reminders relevant

**Notice:** Generic reminders do not reconnect the buyer with the purpose.

**Change:** Use the agreed issue, participants, and preparation in a concise truthful reminder.

**Suggested wording:** “The next conversation is about [agreed issue]. Please bring [relevant preparation].”

**Next branch:** If the buyer cannot attend, offer a reschedule; if opted out, stop.

**Practice check:** A reminder contains a real reason to attend, not fake urgency.

**Boundary:** Do not make automated messages pretend to be live personal outreach.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 421–550; V1 lines 1–622 (0.0–1298.3s); V2 lines 3967–4725 (7800.6–9138.6s); V2 lines 7001–7766 (13624.4–15188.8s)



## OPS05 — Handle no-shows without inventing intent

**Notice:** A missed meeting is labeled disinterest or incompetence.

**Change:** Check whether the buyer wants to reschedule and make the purpose clear.

**Suggested wording:** “The meeting was missed. Would another time still be useful for [agreed issue]?”

**Next branch:** If yes, agree a time; if no or opted out, stop.

**Practice check:** Record the observed no-show separately from any stated reason.

**Boundary:** Do not infer a psychological explanation from nonattendance.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 421–550; V1 lines 1–622 (0.0–1298.3s); V2 lines 3967–4725 (7800.6–9138.6s); V2 lines 7001–7766 (13624.4–15188.8s)



## OPS06 — Maintain useful call notes

**Notice:** Notes contain vague labels but omit commitments and corrections.

**Change:** Capture confirmed problem, changes, objection, promises, owner, and next step.

**Suggested wording:** “The note should say what was learned and what was agreed, not simply interested.”

**Next branch:** If a fact was corrected, replace the premise in the summary; if uncertain, label it.

**Practice check:** Another rep can continue without contradicting the buyer.

**Boundary:** Drafting notes does not authorize a CRM write.

## OPS07 — Keep follow-up purposeful

**Notice:** Pipeline contact repeats checking in without new relevance.

**Change:** Use the unresolved question or agreed date as the reason for contact.

**Suggested wording:** “The open question was support after delivery. The verified answer is now available.”

**Next branch:** If the issue is resolved, ask the appropriate next step; if contact is declined, stop.

**Practice check:** Each follow-up references an agreed issue or useful new fact.

**Boundary:** Persistence is not permission to ignore opt-outs.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 421–550; V1 lines 1–622 (0.0–1298.3s); V2 lines 3967–4725 (7800.6–9138.6s); V2 lines 7001–7766 (13624.4–15188.8s)



## OPS08 — Inspect qualification before blaming closers

**Notice:** A team filters almost everyone before the close or sends clearly unsuitable leads.

**Change:** Review actual fit criteria and downstream outcomes, not just closer convenience.

**Suggested wording:** “Which rejected leads lacked a real requirement, and which were rejected before the offer was understood?”

**Next branch:** If criteria are too restrictive, propose a measured test; if fit is poor, improve targeting.

**Practice check:** Use qualified throughput and outcome evidence to evaluate the change.

**Boundary:** Do not conceal material pricing to manufacture appointments.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 421–550; V1 lines 1–622 (0.0–1298.3s); V2 lines 3967–4725 (7800.6–9138.6s); V2 lines 7001–7766 (13624.4–15188.8s)



## OPS09 — Separate staffing from process bottlenecks

**Notice:** The team wants more hires while current capacity or conversion is unclear.

**Change:** Measure available slots, utilized time, show rate, and actual qualified demand.

**Suggested wording:** “Is the bottleneck too few available conversations, too few shows, or what happens during the call?”

**Next branch:** If capacity is full, assess coverage; if not, fix the observed bottleneck.

**Practice check:** Recommend a change against a measured constraint.

**Boundary:** Do not import historical commission rates or hiring quotas as universal rules.

## OPS10 — Align incentives with useful outcomes

**Notice:** A team optimizes easy closes, booked calls, or claimed revenue at the expense of total results.

**Change:** Review show quality, qualification, collected cash, delivery fit, and customer outcomes together.

**Suggested wording:** “The metric should reward a useful completed step, not merely a label change.”

**Next branch:** If gaming is evident, redesign the measure for review; if not, do not infer misconduct.

**Practice check:** Separate booked contract, promised payment, collected cash, and later refunds.

**Boundary:** Compensation redesign needs business context; a call audit cannot prescribe it alone.
