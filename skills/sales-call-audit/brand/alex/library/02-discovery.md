# Discovery that changes the recommendation

Original coaching adaptations. Suggested dialogue is newly written, not a source quotation. Source ranges identify the reviewed teaching sections supporting this module. Each card has a distinct trigger; apply only when the call evidence fits.



## DIS01 — Find the reason for the conversation

**Notice:** The buyer asks for information but no useful objective is known.

**Change:** Ask what the information is meant to help them decide or improve.

**Suggested wording:** “What would this information help the business change?”

**Next branch:** If a specific goal emerges, follow it; if exploratory, offer an appropriate brief overview.

**Practice check:** Get a buyer-stated reason without assigning a problem to them.

**Boundary:** Some buyers are legitimately researching.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 812–921; V1 lines 1680–1835 (3507.6–3818.5s); V2 lines 5102–5228 (9882.5–10099.6s)



## DIS02 — Make the current state concrete

**Notice:** The rep labels the situation without an example or baseline.

**Change:** Ask what happens today in the part of the business being discussed.

**Suggested wording:** “Walk through the last time that happened. What did the team do?”

**Next branch:** If an example is available, use it; if not, keep the diagnosis tentative.

**Practice check:** Capture one observable current-state example.

**Boundary:** Do not demand irrelevant revenue numbers for every sale.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 812–921; V1 lines 1680–1835 (3507.6–3818.5s); V2 lines 5102–5228 (9882.5–10099.6s)



## DIS03 — Define the desired change

**Notice:** The buyer says grow, improve, or scale without describing the change.

**Change:** Ask what would be different if the problem were solved.

**Suggested wording:** “What would be happening differently three months from now if this worked?”

**Next branch:** If measurable, record the measure; if qualitative, record the concrete behavior.

**Practice check:** Restate the desired result without adding a target the buyer never supplied.

**Boundary:** A desired deadline is not a guaranteed delivery date.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 812–921; V1 lines 1680–1835 (3507.6–3818.5s); V2 lines 5102–5228 (9882.5–10099.6s)



## DIS04 — Find the obstacle

**Notice:** Current and desired states are known but the rep jumps to a pitch.

**Change:** Ask what currently prevents the change.

**Suggested wording:** “What is stopping the team from doing that now?”

**Next branch:** If the obstacle is known, test fit; if uncertain, examine a recent example.

**Practice check:** Connect the recommendation to a confirmed obstacle.

**Boundary:** Do not presume that taking a call proves the offer is needed.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 812–921; V1 lines 1680–1835 (3507.6–3818.5s); V2 lines 5102–5228 (9882.5–10099.6s)



## DIS05 — Replace vague labels with an example

**Notice:** The buyer says systems, people, or strategy and the rep echoes the label.

**Change:** Ask for one incident that shows what the label means in practice.

**Suggested wording:** “When the owner is away, what is the first thing that stops working?”

**Next branch:** If people conflict, explore responsibility; if reporting fails, explore reporting.

**Practice check:** Use the example to change the next question.

**Boundary:** Do not repeat the same abstract question louder.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 812–921; V1 lines 1680–1835 (3507.6–3818.5s); V2 lines 5102–5228 (9882.5–10099.6s)



## DIS06 — Accept a corrected diagnosis

**Notice:** The buyer corrects the rep's premise and the rep repeats it.

**Change:** Say what changed in the understanding and ask the next question using the correction.

**Suggested wording:** “The reports do arrive. The concern is what happens without direct supervision. What breaks first?”

**Next branch:** If confirmed, investigate execution; if corrected again, let the buyer explain before labeling.

**Practice check:** Make the next two questions consistent with the correction.

**Boundary:** A polite yes before a correction does not override the correction.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 812–921; V1 lines 1680–1835 (3507.6–3818.5s); V2 lines 5102–5228 (9882.5–10099.6s)



## DIS07 — Investigate prior attempts

**Notice:** A previous provider or internal fix failed.

**Change:** Ask what was tried, for how long, what worked, and what remained missing.

**Suggested wording:** “What improved with that approach, and where did it still fall short?”

**Next branch:** If it worked partly, preserve that gain; if failure cause is unknown, do not diagnose it as fact.

**Practice check:** Name the missing component using evidence, not a competitor insult.

**Boundary:** Do not claim every previous provider failed for the same reason.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 812–921; V1 lines 1680–1835 (3507.6–3818.5s); V2 lines 5102–5228 (9882.5–10099.6s)



## DIS08 — Quantify only with real inputs

**Notice:** The rep invents the cost of a problem or asserts a huge return.

**Change:** Ask for the buyer's relevant estimate and distinguish it from a measured figure.

**Suggested wording:** “Roughly how often does that happen, and what does each instance cost the team?”

**Next branch:** If reliable figures exist, calculate transparently; if not, use the stated operational impact.

**Practice check:** Label every number as measured, estimated, or unknown.

**Boundary:** Do not equate all revenue at risk with profit lost.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 812–921; V1 lines 1680–1835 (3507.6–3818.5s); V2 lines 5102–5228 (9882.5–10099.6s)



## DIS09 — Recap before the recommendation

**Notice:** The rep moves from scattered facts to an offer without confirming the problem.

**Change:** Summarize current state, desired change, and obstacle in the buyer's language.

**Suggested wording:** “The team can sell, but new hires cannot repeat the process. Is that the main issue to solve?”

**Next branch:** If yes, map the offer; if no, revise the recap.

**Practice check:** The buyer confirms or corrects a concise recap before the pitch.

**Boundary:** Do not force three problems when one clear problem is sufficient.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 812–921; V1 lines 1680–1835 (3507.6–3818.5s); V2 lines 5102–5228 (9882.5–10099.6s)



## DIS10 — Stop discovery when it is sufficient

**Notice:** Repeated pain questions add no new information and delay the useful next step.

**Change:** Name what is already understood and ask permission to explain the relevant solution.

**Suggested wording:** “The bottleneck is clear enough to discuss an option. Would it help to see how the service addresses it?”

**Next branch:** If ready, transition; if a material gap remains, ask only that question.

**Practice check:** Explain why the next question is necessary or omit it.

**Boundary:** More distress is not the objective of discovery.

**Teaching sources:** H1 lines 812–921; V1 lines 1680–1835 (3507.6–3818.5s); V2 lines 5102–5228 (9882.5–10099.6s)
